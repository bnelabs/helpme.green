# Material-handling framework

This is the plain-language orientation framework used by the public onboarding page in
[`website/`](../website/). It helps a new user make a material question safer and more useful
before asking for a route or recommendation. It is not a substitute for a site procedure, a
professional assessment, a laboratory result, or current legal/HSE requirements.

## Five moves

1. **Pause and protect.** If a sample is hazardous, pressurised, contaminated, unknown, or unsafe
   to handle, stop. Do not open, test, move, or process it just to gather information. Follow the
   appropriate site procedure or contact the responsible professional.
2. **Describe.** Record what is observable: appearance, form, condition, origin, attachments, and
   what is happening to the item. Keep an observation separate from a material name.
3. **Separate uncertainty.** Mark what is known, suspected, assumed, and still open. Do not turn a
   reference example or an AI first read into a confirmed identity.
4. **Compare carefully.** Use relevant library examples and source passages as context. Preserve
   source identity, scope, jurisdiction, licence note, fetch status, and limitations with the note.
5. **Verify before acting.** Choose the next appropriate check: a label or record, a suitable test,
   a professional review, a site requirement, a legal/regulatory check, or a route-specific
   specification. The assistant may suggest what to verify; it does not authorize the action.

## Boundaries

helpme.green can organize observations, compare relevant references, surface uncertainty, and help
write a next-check question. It cannot prove what a particular sample is, clear a material for HSE or
legal purposes, confirm a machine's capability, guarantee a yield/price/business outcome, or execute
purchasing, shipment, production, or other physical actions.

The material library is contextual rather than diagnostic. Local notes and photos remain subject to
the runtime's browser/provider boundaries; do not upload sensitive material or credentials to a
provider without a deliberate, configured decision.
